# 2026-10-26: Xet's dedup between two releases, measured offline: about a quarter of the published bytes, none of the group files

#188 modelled that about 55% of a release's upload would deduplicate against the release
before under Xet (44–62% across page sizes). Its reasoning: the published files use
Parquet content-defined chunking (CDC), so an untouched page re-encodes to the same bytes.
It also said a full release with today's writer gets the same dedup. If that held, every
slow-link figure in the campaign (all modelled at 7 MB/s with no dedup) would be
pessimistic by about half.

This entry tests that offline, with Xet's own chunker and no Hub:
- two releases of the bench slice, written by the real pipeline;
- every published and group file of both chunked;
- the dedup simulated, and checked against hf_xet 1.6.0 itself uploading to a local
  content-addressed store on disk.

The scripts, the chunker's test vectors and the result tables are in
`2026-10-26-xet-dedup/` (see Reproduce). Measured and modelled figures are kept apart:
everything on the slice is **measured**; every release-scale figure and wall is
**modelled**.

- **Why a release dedups much less than #188 modelled** (each measured on the slice):
  1. **Every row group moves.**
     - A part's row groups are cut every `row_group_rows` rows from its start, and that
       count is recomputed from each release's own rows (main claims: 155,278 rows a row
       group, then 155,266).
     - A key's parts are equal rows.
     - So one row more or less anywhere before a row group shifts it.
  2. **A shifted row group keeps only about a third of its bytes,** even when none of its
     rows changed: 33.3% of the scholarly claims' (Xet chunk level).
     - **Dictionary-encoded column chunks re-encode entirely:** the dictionary follows the
       order values first appear in. They keep 4.5% of their 4.6 MB.
     - **The first and last page of every column chunk change,** and with them every
       column of only a page or two.
     - **The plain columns' inner pages do realign** (CDC works there): they keep 56.6%
       of their 6.7 MB.
     - An aligned untouched row group dedups 99.8–100%. #188 put this effect at ~0.5%.
  3. **At a release, the language tables' pages are large next to their churn.**
     - A page holds 16k–50k rows of one language, and a week touches 0.007–0.03% of a
       language's rows.
     - In a 4M-row table at those rates, written by the real writer, 23–47% dedups
       (labels, descriptions, links) with the edits clustered as #188 measured, and only
       4–14% if they are uniform.
     - Aliases dedup 1–5%.
  4. **Xet's chunks are larger than the claims' and descriptions' compressed pages**
     (~37 KB on average against 64 KiB). A changed page takes its neighbours' bytes with
     it in its Xet chunk.
     - For example, scholarly descriptions keep 89% of their pages but 74% of their Xet
       chunks.
  5. **The group files are written without CDC** (only compact.py's writers use it):
     - fixed 122,880-row row groups;
     - rows in dump order;
     - chunk boundaries that move with every entity created or deleted before them.

     Nothing after a group file's first change realigns.

## 1. Xet's chunker, and the dedup a client does (from source)

xet-core at tag v1.6.0 (de71453), the one `uv.lock` pins as hf-xet 1.6.0. Below, a path
is in that tree. The gearhash crate is 0.1.3, its version in `Cargo.lock`; the
downloaded crate's sha256 matches the lock's checksum.

- **Constants** (`xet_core_structures/src/xorb_object/constants.rs:5–22`):
  - **chunks:** `TARGET_CHUNK_SIZE` 64 KiB, `MINIMUM_CHUNK_DIVISOR` 8 (8 KiB),
    `MAXIMUM_CHUNK_MULTIPLIER` 2 (128 KiB);
  - **xorbs:** `MAX_XORB_BYTES` 64 MiB, `MAX_XORB_CHUNKS` 8,192.
- **Boundaries** (`xet_data/src/deduplication/chunking.rs:29–139`):
  - **The hash:** gearhash, `hash = (hash << 1) + TABLE[byte]`, with the crate's
    `DEFAULT_TABLE` (gearhash `src/scalar.rs`, `src/table.rs`).
  - **The mask:** `(target − 1) << leading_zeros(target − 1)`, the top 16 bits. A boundary
    is after a byte where `hash & mask == 0`.
  - **Per chunk:** the hash restarts at 0, and the first `min − 64 − 1` bytes are skipped
    unhashed. A match counts only at `min` bytes or more, and the chunk is cut at `max`.
  - **Streaming:** the chunker's state carries over between blocks, so it cuts where the
    whole buffer is cut (xet-core's own `test_chunk_boundaries`).
- **Hashes** (`xet_core_structures/src/merklehash/`):
  - **A chunk:** BLAKE3 keyed with `DATA_KEY` (`data_hash.rs:272–297`).
  - **A xorb or file:** the aggregated hash. Nodes are merged in groups of 2–9, cut where
    `hash % 4 == 0` (the last u64 word, `data_hash.rs:123`). Each group is the lines
    `"{hex} : {size}\n"` hashed with `INTERNAL_NODE_HASH`. The file hash keys that root
    again with a zero salt (`aggregated_hashes.rs:1–195`).
- **Dedup, per file** (`file_deduplication.rs:80–298`):
  - **What is searched:** each 8 MiB block's chunks (`processing/constants.rs:31`) are
    looked up in the shards the client knows, in this order:
    - the resumed session;
    - this session;
    - the local shard cache;
    - any shard a global dedup query fetched.
  - **A dedup range** is a run of consecutive chunks of one xorb
    (`shard_format.rs:673–757`). The file's own pending xorb is searched too.
  - **The fragmentation guard** (`defrag_prevention.rs`; `xet_runtime/.../deduplication.rs`)
    can refuse a short range when the last 128 ranges average under 4–8 chunks.
- **Global dedup:**
  - **What is queried:** a file's first chunk, and any chunk with `hash % 1024 == 0`, at
    least 256 chunks after the file's last query (`file_deduplication.rs:156–176`;
    `metadata_shard/constants.rs:8`; `processing/constants.rs:6`).
  - **What the Hub indexes:** each uploaded shard's file-first chunks and its eligible
    chunks (`streaming_shard.rs:457–500`).
  - **A hit** returns a shard (`GET /v1/chunks/default-merkledb/{hash}`,
    `openapi/cas.openapi.yaml:117–141`). What that shard holds is the Hub's choice.
    xet-core's LocalClient returns every xorb of the shard holding the chunk
    (`local_client.rs:1480–1506`).
- **The local shard cache:**
  - **What goes in:** after an upload, each session shard moves into the cache with a
    3-week expiry (`shard_interface/native.rs:297–320`;
    `metadata_shard/constants.rs:16`).
  - **Its size:** 16 GB at most (`xet_runtime/.../shard.rs:30`).
  - **So on the machine that uploaded release A,** within 3 weeks, release B finds every
    chunk of A with no query.

**The port** (`xetchunk.c`, `xet.py`):
- **The chunker** is about 40 lines of C, the gear table generated from the crate's
  `table.rs`.
- **The hashes and the dedup** are in Python with the `blake3` package. `xet.Uploader` is
  `FileDeduper`, line for line: the two query passes, the dedup ranges, the pending-xorb
  lookup, the guard, xorb cuts at 64 MiB or 8,192 chunks, and the metrics' own
  accounting.

**Verification** (`test_xet.py`, `vectors.py`, `test_vectors.json`):
- **xet-core's own reference vectors**, extracted from its test source:
  - `test_correctness_1mb_random_data` (SplitMix64 data, its sample bytes checked first);
  - `test_correctness_1mb_const_data`;
  - all 128 cases of `test_correctness_100kb_hitting_data`;
  - the 18 node lists of `aggregated_hashes.rs`'s `test_correctness`, "intended to be used
    as a reference to ensure that other implementations or ports of these functions
    produce the correct hashes": their xorb hash, and their file hash under 3 salts;
  - the hex form.

  **130 chunker, 72 merkle and 1 hex vector: 0 failed.**
- **Against hf_xet 1.6.0 itself:** `hash_files` on 12 files, 224.1 MB: the 4 bench chunk
  files, B1's published main and scholarly claims, and B1's 6 claims group files. **The
  port's file hash equals hf_xet's for every one.**
- **The simulator against the client,** on every release pair (§3): the same figure to
  0.1 pp.

- **Row groups don't realign.**
  - **Their cut:** `sort_by_id.write_parts` cuts every `row_group_rows` rows from a part's
    start.
  - **That count moves too:** `row_group_rows` is recomputed from each release's decoded
    bytes. B1's main claims went from 155,278 to 155,266 rows a row group, so every main
    claims row group moved even before any insertion.
- **Pages realign, within a plain column chunk.** In a shifted scholarly row group,
  `statement_id` (2.26 MB, 10 pages) changed its first and last page only.
- **Dictionary-encoded column chunks don't realign.** A shift reorders the dictionary, so
  every index changes. In B1's shifted untouched scholarly row groups:
  - the dictionary-encoded columns (references' hashes, snaks, qualifiers: 41% of the
    bytes) keep 4.5%;
  - the plain columns keep 56.6%.
- **A touched row group** is about as bad as a shifted one (25–34%): its dictionaries take
  the new values, and the rows after the edit shift.
- **The language tables at a release's size** (`writer_shift.py`; the slice's are too
  small):
  - **The test:** a 4M-row table of each kind (2 row groups, the real writer, the slice's
    own values), with each table's measured weekly churn applied: the share of its rows
    touched at 1× (labels 0.0097%, descriptions 0.021%, aliases 0.029%, links 0.0071%),
    84–96% of them adding or removing a row.

    sweeps within 4 of 32 windows).

  | descriptions | 1× | 4.0% | 23.2% | 24.2% | 49.7% |
  | aliases | 1× | 1.1% | 5.1% | 5.3% | 25.9% |
  | links (titles) | 1× | 13.9% | 47.0% | 46.9% | 72.3% |

  - **Their row groups shift too, but cost little:** their columns are plain (pq-glue
    doesn't dictionary-encode a column whose values are over 3/4 distinct), so their pages

  - **Their pages are the limit:** a page of 16k–50k rows almost always holds an edit.

### Footers, metadata and statistics

- **Nothing in them depends on the release:** B0's files are byte-identical to A's.
- **They change whenever any page does,** through the page offsets and the column index's
  per-page min and max. But they are small:
  - 0.3% of the main claims file and 0.6% of the scholarly one;
  - 0.03–0.06% of the 4M-row language tables (0.2–0.3% with 32–128 KiB pages);
  - so ≤0.6% of a release's bytes, at a release's part sizes.

### Part boundaries

- **Master's part boundaries move, like row groups:**
  - an in-memory key's parts are equal rows (`KeyPlan.part_rows`), so any change moves
    every later boundary;
  - a bucketed key's parts are packs of buckets cut at row counts.
- **So every part after a key's first change starts shifted:** its row groups shifted,
  and its footer new.
- **#180's design B** (parts by id region) would fix the part boundaries. Its one-time
  boundary change would cost one release about what master loses every week. But the row
  groups inside a part would still shift under master's rule.
- **What keeps an unchanged row group's bytes** is content-defined row groups as well
  (below). The slice's files are one part a key, so this is from the code, not measured.

### A layout that would keep alignment (counterfactual, measured on the slice; not implemented)

**The layout:** row groups cut at an entity boundary, at least half the target rows in,
whose id's hash is 0 mod M (fixed for the table), or at twice the target. Same writer,
same settings (`writer_shift.py`).

- **On the slice's claims, B1 against A:**
  - scholarly claims 73.9% against master's 58.7% (page level 87.5% against 61.4%);
  - main claims 13.7% against 10.7%.
- **On the language tables:** no change (their loss is the pages').

## 5. At a release (modelled; `project.py`)

a row group, or a page, match a release's:

- **Scholarly claims:**
  - **Release density:** ~4.6k entities a row group (99,469 rows at ~21.5 rows an entity;

## 7. What remains to confirm on the Hub, and how to check it without new Hub calls

**What only a real release shows:**

1. **Whether release A's shards reach release B's client.**
   - **On the machine that uploaded A:** within 3 weeks and under the cache's 16 GB, they
     are in its local shard cache (`HF_XET_CACHE`, default `~/.cache/huggingface/xet`).
     The figures above are that case, and a release's shards are ~0.2 GB (about 50
     bytes a chunk here), so it holds.
     Check that nothing clears that directory between releases.
   - **Otherwise** (another machine, or the cache cleared), only global dedup finds A, and
     what the Hub returns for a hit is the Hub's choice:
     - the whole session shard gives the "cache emptied" figures above;
     - the xorb alone gives nearly the same here.
2. **Nothing else on the Hub changes what is sent:**
   - the dedup is decided client-side, before any byte is sent;
   - the Hub's only part is the global query's answer;
   - xorb compression saves ~0.2% on zstd pages.
3. **The release scale**, which the slice can only stand in for (§5): the churn per
   language and per row group, and the large languages' pages.

**How the user can check, with no new Hub API use.** hf_xet logs every uploaded file's
dedup:
- **Where:** `~/.cache/huggingface/xet/logs` by default (`HF_XET_LOG_DEST`, or
  `$HF_XET_CACHE/logs`, `$HF_HOME/xet/logs`).
- **How:** one JSON-lines file a process, at INFO, kept 14 days and up to 250 MB
  (`xet_runtime/src/config/groups/log.rs`; `logging/init.rs`).
- **The line, after each file is chunked and deduplicated**
  (`xet_data/src/processing/file_cleaner.rs:274–282`):

  ```
  {"timestamp":"...","level":"INFO","fields":{"action":"clean","file_name":"<local path>",
   "file_size_count":<bytes>,"new_bytes_count":<bytes sent>,"start_ts":"...","end_processing_ts":"..."},...}
  ```

  - **Its fields:** `new_bytes_count` is the bytes that went into new xorbs, what was
    sent (before LZ4, ~nothing on zstd pages). The rest was deduplicated.
  - **One quirk:** `file_size_count` (`total_bytes`) counts a range the fragmentation
    guard refused twice (`file_deduplication.rs:200–203, 236–237`), so the file's own size
    is better when it still exists.
  - **Checked here:** the line and its fields come from hf_xet 1.6.0's own logs in the
    local-store runs. Their totals give the same 33.5% as the client's metrics.

**What to run on the release machine, after the next release's uploads** (a release of
20261026, say, uploaded within 3 weeks of 20261019's from the same machine):

```
python docs/journal/2026-10-26-xet-dedup/xet_logs.py              # the default log directory
python docs/journal/2026-10-26-xet-dedup/xet_logs.py --log-dir DIR --out xet_dedup.json
```

**What it gives:**
- **per process** (a run's pushes, each finalise run's commits): GB, GB sent, dedup;
- **by release, kind, set and table:** group files (`releases/<r>[-scholar]/staging/...`)
  and published files (anything else under `releases/`).

**What to compare against:**
- this entry's modelled ~25% for the published files and ~0% for the group files;
- #188's 55%;
- the "cache emptied" figures, if the release ran on another machine.

**#187's `throughput_measure.py logs`** reads only the concurrency controller's lines.
`throughput_measure.logs.patch` (against #187's head, 2289fab) adds these, through
`xet_logs.read_dedup`. Applied and run on real hf_xet logs here, it gives the same table.
It is left for #187's PR to take, since that file isn't on master.

## Verdict

**Negative for #188's premise, as stated.**
- **Xet dedups ~25% of a release's published bytes,** not 55%, with today's writer.
- **It dedups ~0% of the group files.**
- **The campaign's 7 MB/s walls are ~10% pessimistic, not ~50%** (master 9h46m against
  10h50m).

**The mechanism #188 relied on works, where it applies:**
- CDC pages realign within a plain column;
- an aligned, untouched row group re-encodes byte for byte;
- the writer is deterministic.

**What defeats it:**
- the layout's fixed-row row groups and parts;
- dictionary encoding;
- pages large next to a language's weekly churn;
- no CDC at all in the group files.

**Not implemented here, for the queue (each changes the published or build-branch bytes,
not the rows):**
1. **Content-defined row groups and parts** in `sort_by_id`: 25% → ~36% of the published
   bytes. A layout change, so it needs parity, the bench and the user.
2. **Smaller CDC pages for the language tables:** → ~42%, at some size cost.
3. **Group files** written with CDC and cut at content-defined boundaries, or no group
   files at all (#180's design C). Today their ~124 GB is sent whole at every release,
   and at slow links it is the bigger share.

**The dedup estimate in #188 should be revised to these figures.** Its incremental
design's gain at fast links is unaffected.
