# The native bz2 decoder's threads in the split (lmmx/mibz2#65)

**Question.** The split's native decoder runs `WIKIDATA_BZ2_THREADS` workers, `os.cpu_count()`
by default (every logical CPU). Is that the right count once the decoder shares the
machine with the split's parse workers and writer pools? mibz2's standalone sweeps put its
knee at 4 workers of the i9-10900K's 10 cores (mibz2 journal, 2026-10-28-workstation-scaling).

## Where the count comes from

- `dump.bz2_threads()`: `WIKIDATA_BZ2_THREADS`, else `os.cpu_count()`.
- `Bz2Decoder(threads)` (reshape/src/bz2_py.rs) hands it to
  `mibz2::StreamDecoder::new(threads, slots = 2 × threads, kernels)`.
- A default inside mibz2 alone would leave this pipeline unchanged. A cap for wikidata-pq
  belongs in `bz2_threads()`, and needs no mibz2 release.
- The split runs beside the decoder:
  - `os.cpu_count() − 2` parse workers (a ThreadPoolExecutor; the reshape releases the GIL);
  - the rayon writer pools, sized to the workers (`_bound_writer_pool`).

  On a 4-vCPU runner that is 4 decoder threads, the scanner, 2 workers and 2 writer
  threads, on 2 physical cores. On the workstation it would be 20 decoder threads and 18
  workers on 10 cores.

## The sweep (bench/bz2threads.py, #210)

- **The split stage:** the CI bench's own `pipeline.split_stage`, run once per count on the
  quick tier's 79.2 MB slice, each run from nothing in a fresh directory.
- **Only the decoder's count changes.** Each run's log is checked for:
  - the decoder actually built and its own thread count (the split's new `bz2:` line);
  - "0 chunks already written";
  - the same 2 parse workers and 2 writer threads as every other run.
- **Arms and order:** `auto`, 1, 2 and 4, for 8 rounds. The order rotates and reverses, so
  each arm runs in each position equally often. On these runners `4` is the same setting
  as `auto`, an A/A arm.
- **Paired:** each round's ratio to `auto`, with a 95% interval.
- **Two runners,** each a sweep of its own (run 40659171345).

## What it shows (measured, these runners only)

- **On a 2-core SMT machine, 2 decoder threads (one per physical core) beat the default 4.**
  - The split's wall is the same within noise on both runners.
  - Its CPU is 7–12% lower and its peak RSS about 3% lower.
- **The split is not bound by the decode here.**
  - On the 7763, 2 threads decode 14% slower alone, yet the split's wall does not move.
  - The two extra SMT threads mostly burn CPU that the parse workers could have used.
- **One thread is too few.** It costs wall on both runners: +5% and +17%.
- **The A/A arm is clean** on both runners, so the paired intervals are believable.

## What it does not show

- **A release-shaped gain.** These runs are the split stage alone, on a 79 MB slice.
  - The split is about 40% of a scaled release's serial wall on a 4-vCPU VM (bench/full/README).
  - A CPU saving in it shows up in a release's wall only where the stages overlap and
    compete for cores.
  - `bz2threads.py --full` (bench-full, overlapped) is the comparison for that.
- **A many-core result.** Whether 2/4/6/8/10 beat `auto` (20) on the 10-core workstation
  needs `just bench-bz2-threads auto,2,4,6,8,10` there. The CI runners cannot answer it.
- **Fine wall differences.** `split_stage` polls its process every 0.2 s, so a split's wall
  is quantised to about 0.2 s (4% of a 4.6 s split). That is part of why the wall intervals
  are ±2–5%. The CPU and RSS figures are not quantised.

## Release-shaped: bench-full, overlapped (run 40664370762)

- **Setup:** `bz2threads.py --full "--release 20261019 --windows 24 --window-mb 25"`, `auto`
  against `2`.
  - Bench-full's overlapped orchestration: the split, then both sets' runs following it
    at nice 10, then finalise's runs, with the network and the Hub off the timed path.
  - The release's group rule, so at this scale each set is one group.
  - 4 balanced rounds on each of two runners, both EPYC 7763 with 2 cores × SMT.
- **Checks:** every run used the requested decoder threads (4 or 2), split from nothing,
  and kept 2 parse workers and 2 writer threads. All 16 runs gave the same published-files
  digest, 3ad0c4aa…, which is also every earlier bench-full record's.

| figure (2 threads vs `auto`) | runner 1 | runner 2 | both, 8 pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| total wall (median, `auto`: 164 s, 167 s) | **−2.0% ±0.9%** | **−1.8% ±1.4%** | **−1.9% ±0.6%** |
| total CPU | −0.6% ±0.8% (noise) | −0.3% ±1.0% (noise) | −0.4% ±0.5% (noise) |
| peak RSS, every process (median, `auto`: 12.0 GB, 11.6 GB) | −3.2% ±6.0% (noise) | −1.7% ±15% (noise) | −2.4% ±5.6% (noise) |
| split stage wall (median, `auto`: 66 s, 68 s) | **−4.4% ±0.6%** | **−4.4% ±1.3%** | **−4.4% ±0.5%** |
| split stage CPU | **−1.4% ±0.4%** | −0.3% ±1.1% (noise) | −0.9% ±0.6% |

- **On this machine class, it is a speedup, not only an efficiency gain.**
  - The split stage is 4.4% faster on both runners. It is about 40% of the wall here.
  - The release-shaped wall is about 3 s (1.9%) shorter, consistent with the split's
    3 s saving carried straight through.
  - Total CPU and peak RSS do not move beyond noise.
- **It differs from the slice sweep.** On the CI slice the split's wall did not move and
  its CPU fell 7–12%. On the real windows the split's wall falls and its CPU barely moves.
  - Two differences may explain it; neither is tested.
  - The windows carry 18.7× JSON per bz2 byte against the slice's 13.7×, so the decode is
    a smaller share of the split.
  - In the overlapped run, the pipeline's processes follow the split and compete for the
    same 4 logical CPUs.
- **Limits:**
  - One CPU model (both runners were 7763s) and 8 pairs.
  - A 0.6 GB scaled sample with one group a set, so the overlap is thinner than at a
    release.
  - A 2-core machine says nothing about the 10-core workstation.

## A hypothesis for the default (not implemented)

Two observations, not a rule:

- **2-core AMD runners:** one decoder thread per physical core (2 of 4) gives the same or a
  shorter split, and a shorter release-shaped wall, than every logical CPU.
- **The 10-core i9, decoding alone:** 4 workers beat 10.

Together they suggest "physical cores, under some cap", but they do not fix a formula
across architectures, block sizes or pipeline configurations. The default is to be
chosen from counts measured on each class of machine (the workstation sweep next), then
implemented in wikidata-pq's `bz2_threads()` alone, with `WIKIDATA_BZ2_THREADS` still
overriding it, and benchmarked again before it merges. No mibz2 change or release is
involved.
